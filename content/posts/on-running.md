I started running seriously about a year ago. Not casually — seriously. Tracking mileage, following a plan, the whole thing. I was skeptical of people who said running changed them. I am now one of those people.

Here's what I've learned.

## Consistency Beats Intensity

The single biggest predictor of my fitness gains wasn't how hard I ran. It was how often I ran. Showing up for a slow, easy 4-miler on a Tuesday when I didn't feel like it compounded into something real over months.

This is deeply unsexy advice. It's also correct.

The body adapts to what you repeatedly ask of it. Two hard runs a week surrounded by rest produces less adaptation than five moderate runs. Volume beats intensity, up to a point.

## The Aerobic Base

Every running plan I've read eventually arrives at the same concept: the aerobic base. The idea is that most of your running should be easy — conversational pace, nose-breathing easy — to build the cardiovascular infrastructure that supports faster running later.

I resisted this. Easy running felt like wasted time. Then I did a four-month block of mostly easy miles and watched my easy pace drop by over a minute per mile without feeling harder.

Now I understand it. You can't build the house before you pour the foundation.

## What Running Has in Common With Software

More than you'd think.

Both reward compound interest. The codebase you maintain daily is healthier than the one you sprint on before a deadline. The fitness you build gradually outperforms the intensity you jam in before a race.

Both punish skipping the boring parts. In software, it's tests and documentation. In running, it's easy miles and recovery. Skipping them feels efficient. It isn't.

Both have feedback loops that operate on different timescales. You don't see the gains from a single run. You don't see the gains from a single refactor. The signal is in the trend, not the data point.

## On Injury

I've had two minor injuries this year. Both happened when I increased mileage too fast. The 10% rule — don't add more than 10% to your weekly mileage — exists for a reason I now understand viscerally rather than theoretically.

Bodies are conservative systems. They adapt, but slowly. Push them faster than they can adapt and something breaks. This is also true of teams, codebases, and organizations.

## What's Next

I'm targeting a half marathon in the fall. The plan is boring: more easy miles, a long run every weekend, a tempo workout once a week. No magic. Just showing up.

That's the whole thing, actually. That's always the whole thing.
